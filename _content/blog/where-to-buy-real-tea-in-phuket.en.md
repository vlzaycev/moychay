You would think good tea is easy to find in Asia – China is not far away. Yet many people who move to Phuket or stay for a season run into the same thing. Supermarkets sell tea bags and bright orange Thai tea with condensed milk, markets sell colourful "premium" tins for tourists. Good everyday loose-leaf tea is surprisingly hard to find.

Here is how real tea differs from souvenir tea, what to look for when you buy, and where in Phuket you can taste tea first and buy it afterwards.

## What real tea is

Real tea is the leaf of the tea plant, picked at a certain time and processed in a certain way. It has an origin, a type and a character. White, green, oolong, red, sheng and shu pu-erh all come from the same plant – the processing is different, and so is the taste.

Good tea needs no sugar or milk. On its own it is smooth, slightly sweet and layered, and the taste changes from one infusion to the next.

## How souvenir tea gives itself away

- **Flavourings.** A loud smell of strawberry, mango or "jasmine" that comes through a closed tin.
- **Dust instead of leaves.** Fine dust and broken bits rather than whole or large leaves.
- **No origin.** A big "premium" on the label, but no word on what the tea is or where it is from.
- **No tasting.** The tin is sold sealed – you cannot smell or brew it first.

The real problem is not the price. People try tea like this, wince and decide tea is "not for them" – when they have simply never had good tea.

## What to look for when you buy

1. **Can you taste it.** The best way to choose tea is to brew it. A seller who pours you a cup before you buy is a good sign.
2. **The dry leaf.** Look at it and smell it: the aroma should be natural, not sweet like candy.
3. **Name and type.** A good seller can tell you what the tea is, where it comes from and how to brew it.
4. **Advice, not upselling.** You should get help choosing for your taste, not the most expensive tin.
