The usual souvenirs from Phuket are dried fruit, coconut oil, spices and fridge magnets. Good tea stands out: people actually drink it instead of putting it away, and it reminds them of the trip long after they are home.

Here is which tea to choose as a gift, how it differs from souvenir tins and how to get it home.

## Why tea makes a good gift

- **It gets used.** Tea is shared with family and friends, not left on a shelf.
- **It is light and compact.** Loose and pressed tea weighs almost nothing and fits in any suitcase.
- **It keeps well.** Dry tea in tight packaging travels without trouble.
- **It can suit the person.** One tea for a coffee lover, another for someone who drinks herbal tea in the evening.

## Which tea to choose

| For | What to give |
|---|---|
| Someone new to tea | A light oolong – soft, floral and sweet |
| Coffee lovers | A dark oolong or red tea – rich, with notes of chocolate and dried fruit |
| Fans of delicate flavours | White tea – notes of honey and dried flowers |
| Tea lovers | A cake of sheng pu-erh, young or aged |
| Someone avoiding caffeine | A herbal blend of herbs, flowers and fruit |

If you are not sure what the person likes, take two or three different teas in small amounts. It is easier to get it right.

## Not only tea – teaware too

For someone who already loves tea, teaware is a great gift: a clay teapot, a gaiwan, a set of cups. At our tea house the teaware and antiques on the shelves are a real collection for sale, and the tea master will help you choose a teapot for a particular tea.

## How to avoid souvenir tea

Bright "premium" tins at markets often hold flavoured tea dust. Before you buy:

1. **Smell the dry leaf.** It should smell natural, not like candy.
2. **Look at the leaf.** Good tea is leaves, not dust.
3. **Ask what it is and where it comes from.** If the seller cannot say, be careful.
4. **Taste it.** The best gift is one you have tried yourself.

More in our guide on [where to buy real tea in Phuket](/blog/where-to-buy-real-tea-in-phuket/).

## How to get it home

- Keep tea in tight, closed packaging, away from spices, coffee and perfume – tea easily picks up smells.
- A pressed pu-erh cake is sturdy and travels well.
- Pack teaware in your hand luggage, wrapped in clothes.
- Check your country's rules on bringing in food before you travel.
