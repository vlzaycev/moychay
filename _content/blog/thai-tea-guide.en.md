"Thai tea" usually means the orange iced drink with condensed milk. But Thailand also has a real tea-growing region in the north and its own herbal drinks you won't find anywhere else in quite the same way. Here is what is worth trying and what to take home.

## Where tea grows in Thailand

The main tea area is in the north, in Chiang Rai province around Doi Mae Salong. Mae Salong was settled by people from China's Yunnan province, and its tea culture is largely Chinese and Taiwanese. Today the hills mostly produce oolongs from Taiwanese cultivars, as well as red and green tea.

Tea does not grow in Phuket, but Thai teas are easy to find in shops and tea houses.

## Five Thai teas worth knowing

| Tea | What it is | Taste |
|---|---|---|
| Mae Salong oolong | Rolled oolong from Taiwanese cultivars | Floral, creamy, slightly sweet |
| GABA oolong | Oolong whose leaves are kept without oxygen before processing | Fruity, with sweet-sour notes |
| Anchan (butterfly pea tea) | Dried butterfly pea flowers, no tea leaf | Mild, herbal, almost neutral |
| Matum (bael tea) | Dried slices of bael fruit, no tea leaf | Slightly sweet, fruity |
| Cha yen | Strong black tea with sugar and condensed milk, usually iced | Sweet, milky |

### Mae Salong oolongs

The best choice if you want Thai tea made from the tea leaf. Light rolled oolongs are soft and floral and can be infused many times. We explain how to brew oolong at home in a [separate guide](/blog/how-to-brew-oolong/).

### GABA oolong

Thailand's most unusual tea. The leaves are kept in an oxygen-free environment before processing, and the result is fruity, with a light tang and notes of dried fruit. More in our guide to [GABA oolong](/blog/gaba-oolong/).

### Anchan

Blue tea made from butterfly pea flowers. The colour is the point: the infusion is bright blue and turns purple with a slice of lemon. It has no caffeine. More in our guide to [butterfly pea tea](/blog/butterfly-pea-tea-anchan/).

### Matum

Dried slices of bael fruit, brewed like a herbal tea and drunk hot or cold. Also caffeine-free.

### Cha yen

The famous orange Thai iced tea: strong black tea with sugar and condensed milk. The bright colour in many ready-made mixes comes from food colouring. Worth trying while you are here, but it is more of a dessert than a tea.

## What to bring home

- **For yourself and tea lovers.** A Mae Salong oolong or GABA oolong.
- **For someone avoiding caffeine.** Anchan or matum.
- **As a fun taste of the holiday.** A pack of cha yen mix.

Loose-leaf tea is light and travels well. Keep it in tight packaging, away from spices and coffee. More tips in [what to bring home from Phuket](/blog/tea-as-a-gift-from-phuket/).
