It's hot in Thailand all year, and a hot cup in the afternoon isn't always what you want. Iced tea bags and sweet Thai milk tea aren't the only options. Real loose-leaf tea can be brewed with cold water, and the result is smooth, naturally sweet and refreshing, with almost no bitterness.

## What cold brew tea is

Cold brew tea is tea steeped in cold water for several hours, usually in the fridge. Cold water pulls flavour from the leaf more slowly and gently than boiling water, so the tea comes out less astringent, with more sweetness and aroma.

## The basic recipe

1. **Tea.** 5–10 g of loose-leaf tea per litre of water. Start with 8 g.
2. **Water.** Bottled or filtered – Thai tap water isn't right for this.
3. **Time.** Pour cold water over the tea, cover it and put it in the fridge.
4. **Strain.** Through a sieve, or just pour it into another jug.

Drink it within a day – cold brew isn't meant to be kept for long.

## How long to steep

| Tea | Time in the fridge |
|---|---|
| White | 6–10 hours |
| Green | 4–8 hours |
| Light oolong | 8–12 hours |
| Dark oolong, GABA | 8–12 hours |
| Sheng pu-erh | 8–12 hours |
| Hibiscus and herbs | 6–10 hours |

The easiest routine: make it in the evening and take out a ready jug in the morning.

## Which tea to choose

### White tea

Probably the best tea for cold brewing: delicate, honeyed and floral. Baihao Yinzhen – silver-haired white buds – is a great choice.

### Green tea

Fresh, grassy and lightly sweet. In cold water it doesn't turn bitter even if you leave it too long. Try Bilochun or jasmine Maofeng.

### Light oolongs

Floral oolongs like Tie Guanyin make a creamy, very aromatic cold brew. It's a frequent pick for guests who have never had Chinese tea before.

### GABA oolong

GABA's fruity, sweet taste opens up well in cold water. More about the tea itself in [GABA oolong: what it is](/blog/gaba-oolong/).

### Hibiscus

For a caffeine-free option, hibiscus gives a bright ruby colour and a tart, berry-like taste. Great over ice with a slice of lime. Another caffeine-free choice is [butterfly pea tea](/blog/butterfly-pea-tea-anchan/).

## Tips

- **Tea over ice.** If you want iced tea right now, brew regular tea twice as strong and pour it over ice.
- **Lime, mint, lemongrass.** Add them to the jug at the end for a Thai-style refresher.
- **Skip the sugar.** Cold brew is naturally sweet; try it without sugar first.
- **Glass.** A glass bottle or jug with a lid is the easiest thing to brew in.

## Cold brew isn't Thai iced tea

Cha yen, the orange Thai tea with condensed milk, is strong black tea with spices, sugar and milk. Cold brew is plain loose-leaf tea with nothing added. More about Thai teas in our [Thai tea guide](/blog/thai-tea-guide/).

## FAQ

### Can I cold brew tea bags?

You can, but loose-leaf tea gives a much cleaner, sweeter taste.

### Can I re-steep the same leaves?

Yes – a good oolong or white tea can be topped up with water once more; the second jug will be lighter.

### Does cold brew tea have caffeine?

Yes, real tea contains caffeine, cold or hot. For a caffeine-free drink choose hibiscus, butterfly pea or herbal blends.
