Blue tea is one of Thailand's best-known drinks. A bright blue infusion that turns purple in front of you makes a great souvenir and a beautiful drink at home. Here is what it is and how to brew it.

## What anchan is

Anchan is not tea in the strict sense. It is the dried flower of butterfly pea (*Clitoria ternatea*), a tropical vine that grows across Southeast Asia. In Thailand it is called nam dok anchan.

There is no tea leaf in it, so there is no caffeine. You can drink it in the evening and serve it to people who don't drink regular tea.

## What it tastes like

Honestly, not much. The taste is mild, herbal and slightly woody. That is why anchan is often blended with other herbs or drunk with honey and lemon. The colour is the main thing.

## Why it changes colour

The blue comes from natural pigments in the flowers, which react to acidity. Add lemon juice, lime or a piece of sour fruit and the drink turns purple or pink. Guests and children love it.

## How to brew it

### Hot

1. Use 5-7 dried flowers per 250 ml cup.
2. Pour over hot water at about 90-95°C.
3. Steep for 3-5 minutes, until the colour is deep.
4. Add honey or a slice of lemon if you like.

### Cold

1. Put 10-15 flowers into a litre of cold water.
2. Leave in the fridge for 4-6 hours.
3. Serve with ice, lime and, if you like, mint.

## What to blend it with

- **Lemongrass.** A classic Thai pairing with a fresh herbal aroma.
- **Honey and lemon.** A soft sweet-sour drink that changes colour as you make it.
- **Green tea or oolong.** For the taste of tea and the colour at the same time.

## Good to know

Anchan is a herbal drink, not a medicine. The internet credits it with many health benefits, but we won't promise any. If you are pregnant, breastfeeding or taking medication, check with your doctor before drinking any herbal tea regularly.
