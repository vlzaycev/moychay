In the last few years matcha in Phuket has become almost as popular as coffee. It is served in Japanese matcha cafés, coffee shops and tea houses. Here is where to drink it, how to tell good matcha from bad and how to make it at home.

## What matcha is

Matcha is green tea ground into a fine powder. With regular tea you steep the leaves and pour off the infusion; matcha is whisked with water and you drink all of it, leaf included. That is why the taste is so full: fresh, grassy, slightly sweet and creamy.

## How to choose matcha

- **Colour.** Good matcha is bright green. A yellow or brownish tint means a lower grade or old powder.
- **Texture.** The powder should be very fine, like dust.
- **Aroma.** Fresh and grassy. A stale smell means it was stored too long or badly.
- **"Ceremonial" and "culinary".** These are marketing labels, not a standard. For straight matcha with water choose a brighter, more expensive one; for lattes and baking a simpler one is fine.

## How to make it at home

1. Sift 2 g of matcha (about half a teaspoon) into a bowl.
2. Add 60-70 ml of water at 70-80°C. Boiling water makes matcha bitter.
3. Whisk with a bamboo chasen in quick zigzag strokes until a fine foam forms.
4. For a latte, add 150-200 ml of milk. Over ice it is a great drink for the heat.

## How to store it in the tropics

Matcha quickly loses colour and aroma to heat, light and humidity. Keep it airtight in the fridge and try to use it within 1-2 months of opening. Let the tin warm to room temperature before opening so no condensation forms inside.
