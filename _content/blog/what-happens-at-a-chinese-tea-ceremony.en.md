A Chinese tea ceremony sounds formal, but at the table it is calm and simple. Someone who knows the tea brews it in front of you, pours it into small cups, and you drink slowly while the taste changes from one infusion to the next. This guide explains what you will see and what you are expected to do – which is very little.

## Gongfu cha in one sentence

*Gongfu cha* (功夫茶) can be translated as "tea made with skill and time". It is a way of brewing Chinese tea – most often oolong and pu-erh – in a small vessel with a generous amount of leaf and many short infusions. Each infusion tastes a little different, and following that change is the whole point.

## The teaware on the table

- **Gaiwan or small teapot.** A lidded bowl or a small clay pot where the leaves are brewed.
- **Fair cup.** A small pitcher. The tea is poured into it first, so every guest gets the same strength.
- **Small cups.** A few sips each – enough to taste, little enough to stay curious about the next infusion.
- **Tea tray.** A tray with drainage, so hot water can be poured over the teaware freely.

## How a ceremony goes, step by step

1. **Choosing the tea.** You say what you like, or simply point at a jar. No tea vocabulary needed.
2. **Warming the teaware.** Hot water is poured over the vessel and the cups, so the tea does not cool down on contact.
3. **Meeting the dry leaf.** The leaves are shown to guests before brewing – it is worth smelling them.
4. **Rinsing the leaf.** For many teas the first very short infusion is poured off. It wakes up compressed or tightly rolled leaves.
5. **Short infusions.** Water goes in for seconds rather than minutes, the tea is poured into the fair cup and then into your cup. This repeats many times as the leaves open.
6. **The end of the tea.** When the infusions become thin, the tea has said everything. Often there is time to sit a little longer.

> You don't need to know any of this in advance. Most of our guests try a tea ceremony for the first time, and questions are part of it.

## What you do as a guest

Hold the cup, smell it, drink slowly. Notice how the second infusion differs from the first, and the fifth from the second. That is all. There are no rules to memorise and no wrong way to hold a cup.

## Gongfu cha compared with a cup of tea

| | Gongfu cha | A mug of tea |
|---|---|---|
| Amount of leaf | Generous | Small |
| Water | Little, in a small vessel | A full mug or pot |
| Infusion time | Seconds | Minutes |
| Number of infusions | Many | One or two |
| What you notice | How the taste changes | One even taste |
