GABA oolong is one of the most unusual teas you can try in Thailand. It is often sold as a "calming tea", but the most interesting thing about it is the taste. Here is what it really is.

## Where the name comes from

GABA is gamma-aminobutyric acid, a compound found in small amounts in the tea leaf. The method that raises its content was developed in Japan in the 1980s. It was later taken up in Taiwan, and today GABA oolong is also made in northern Thailand, in the hills of Chiang Rai province.

## How it is made

Regular oolong is withered, partly oxidised and dried after picking. GABA tea has an extra step: the fresh leaves are kept for several hours in an oxygen-free environment, usually nitrogen. During that time GABA builds up in the leaf and the flavour changes. After that the leaves are processed like a regular oolong: rolled and dried.

## What it tastes like

GABA oolong is easy to recognise. It has a fruity tang, notes of dried fruit, baked apple and honey, sometimes bready notes. The liquor is usually amber. Many people say it tastes like no other oolong.

## What about the health claims

Producers and sellers link GABA tea with relaxation and better sleep. There is little research on this so far, and we won't promise any effects. Drink GABA oolong because it tastes good. It contains caffeine, like any tea made from the tea leaf.

## How to brew it

GABA oolong opens up best in a gaiwan or small teapot.

1. Use 5-7 g of tea per 100 ml.
2. Water at 95-100°C.
3. Rinse the leaves quickly with hot water and pour it off.
4. First infusions 10-20 seconds, then add 5-10 seconds each time.
5. The leaves give 5-7 infusions.

In a mug: 3-4 g per 250-300 ml, 2-3 minutes. More in [how to brew oolong at home](/blog/how-to-brew-oolong/).
